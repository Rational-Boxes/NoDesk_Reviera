Openings

Openings are holes without a door or window: a rectangular opening through a wall, a vertical opening through a floor, ceiling or roof, and a shaft through several floors. The tools are on Architecture › Opening (and the Architecture menu).

Wall openings

  1. Click Architecture › Opening › Wall Opening Wall Opening.
  2. Set the Width and Height in Properties if 1000 × 2100 is not what you want.
  3. Hover a wall in a plan or a 3D view: the opening's outline follows the cursor along the wall, kept inside it. Click to cut it. The tool repeats until Esc.

A wall opening goes through the wall's whole thickness, square to its line, in straight and curved walls. It may not overlap the wall's doors, windows or other openings.

Select it in a plan by its outline. Properties sets its Base Constraint and Base Offset, its Top Constraint (a level, or Unconnected with a height) and Top Offset, its Width, and its place From Wall Start or From Wall End. An opening whose top follows a level grows when the level is raised. Moving it slides it along its wall; it moves and is copied with its wall, and is deleted with it. opening.create_wall, opening.edit_wall

Vertical openings

  1. Open the plan of the level (a ceiling plan to cut a ceiling).
  2. Click Architecture › Opening › Vertical Vertical. Sketch mode starts with the Rectangle tool; lines, arcs and circles work too (see Sketch mode).
  3. Click Finish Edit Mode. The opening cuts the floor on the level that the sketch overlaps most (in a ceiling plan, the ceiling); if no floor is there, the roof it overlaps.

The opening's sides are plumb, whatever the slope: in a roof it makes a skylight-shaped hole with vertical sides. A loop partly outside a floor notches the floor's edge. In a roof, an opening must lie within one roof face (not across a ridge or hip).

Stairs also make vertical openings: when a stair passes under a floor or ceiling that leaves it short of the building code's headroom, Reviera offers to open it. See Stairs. opening.create_vertical

Shafts

  1. Open the plan of the shaft's lowest level.
  2. Click Architecture › Opening › Shaft Shaft and sketch its outline.
  3. Click Finish Edit Mode. The shaft runs from this level up to the next one.

A shaft cuts every floor, ceiling and roof whose body its height reaches into, including ones added later; walls are not cut. Select it in a plan of any level it passes and set its base and top in Properties (for example, a top level higher up). Properties also lists what it cuts. opening.create_shaft, opening.edit_shaft

In plans

Vertical openings and shafts are drawn as their outline crossed corner to corner, on the plans of their host's level (a shaft on every level it passes). Wall openings show as the gap in the cut wall, with their outline. Click the outline or the cross to select one.

Reshaping

  1. Select a vertical opening or shaft in a plan.
  2. Drag a corner grip to move the corner, or an edge grip to move that edge across.

Each drag is one undo step. A vertical opening moves with its floor, ceiling or roof and is deleted with it. opening.set_loops

In IFC

Each opening is an IfcOpeningElement voiding its host, whose own body stays whole (a roof's body is written with its holes). A shaft is an IfcBuildingElementProxy (PROVISIONFORVOID) with the whole prism as its body, and each floor, ceiling or roof it cuts gets its own opening.

Not yet available: openings by face (at right angles to a sloped roof), openings in arched roofs and domes, and guards placed round an opening by themselves (a railing can be sketched along its edge).

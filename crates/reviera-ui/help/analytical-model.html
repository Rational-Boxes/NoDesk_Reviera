The analytical model and its checks

The analytical model is what the analysis sees: members along the framing's axes, panels for walls and slabs, nodes where they meet, and supports. It is kept apart from the physical model, associated with it, and made from it on request. Everything is on the Analyze tab.

Seeing it

Show Analytical draws it over 3D views: members red, nodes blue, panels magenta with a diagonal, supports green, loads orange.

From the physical model

  • Physical to Analytical makes a member for each structural column, beam, brace and truss member, and a panel for each bearing wall and structural floor, associated with them. Nodes merge at the joints, and members are split where others frame into their span (a beam into a girder, a floor at a column), so the frame is connected.
  • Analytical to Physical gives changed section types back to the beams and columns.
  • Disconnect removes the associations; Merge Nodes merges nodes closer than the merge distance.
  • Adjust moves analytical nodes by hand in a 3D view, without moving any physical element: click a node, then where it goes. It stays at its height (X, Y or Z keeps it on that axis), snaps onto other nodes, and the members using it follow in the preview; Enter with a node picked types the move in millimetres. Enter finishes every move as one undo step, a node dropped on another joining it; Esc cancels them. Moved nodes are flagged as manually adjusted.
  • Reset puts the selected analytical elements (or those of the selected physical elements) back where their physical elements put them, keeping their sections, releases, loads and supports.

Selected analytical members, panels and nodes show their own rows in Properties: a member's role, material, start and end releases (Fixed or Pinned) and Analyze As; a panel's role, thickness, material, Analyze As and cladding; a node's support. With Show Analytical on, click them in a 3D view (a node, a member's line or a panel's edge), or select them from the Analytical Checks pane or Results Explorer's Insights.

Panels

Panel by Boundary sketches a flat panel at a plan's level; Panel by Extrusion and Curved Panel click a line or an arc that rises to the next level. Analytical Opening, Split Panel and Cladding (loads but no stiffness) complete them.

Checking it

Check Supports and Check Consistency open the Analytical Checks pane, listing issues by severity with the distance measured against its tolerance:

S1A member or panel with nothing carrying it (a column's foot, a beam's ends).
S2Members carrying one another without reaching a support.
S3No supports at all (an error).
C1Nodes that look connected but are not; an end on another member's span with no node there; a free end; a load off its host.
C2A part with no support: the solver would see a mechanism (an error).
C3, C4Analytical elements away from, or outside, their physical ones.
C5Elements without a counterpart.
C6A beam or wall under a slab whose analytical elements do not meet.
C7, C8Instability hints; missing sections or materials (C8 is off by default).

Select an issue to select its elements; Zoom To frames it; Ignore keeps it out of the list, with a reason, until one of its elements changes. Errors hold Run Analysis. Open the Structural model with problems sample to see every issue, each labelled in its plan.

Rooms

A room fills a region enclosed by walls. It measures its area and volume to the walls' finish faces, follows the walls when they move, and is labelled in plans.

To place rooms

  1. Open the plan of the level. Architecture › Room & Area › Room (RM). Room
  2. Hover inside a region: the region the room would take is shown.
  3. Click to place the room. Click more regions; Esc ends.

Place Rooms Automatically puts a room in every enclosed region of the plan's level that has none, numbered in reading order. Place Rooms Automatically room.place, room.place_automatically

Boundaries

  • Rooms measure to the walls' finish faces, curved walls included.
  • Islands, such as a free-standing wall inside the room, are left out of the area.
  • When the walls move, the room follows them. If the walls open up so the region is no longer enclosed, the room's status becomes Not Enclosed.

Names, numbers and properties

Select a room by clicking its label or its inside in a plan. In Properties:

NameThe room's name (Living, Kitchen).
NumberShown as the element's Name in the identity rows at the top.
Level, Upper Limit, Limit Offset, Base OffsetThe room's height range.
Area, Perimeter, Volume, StatusComputed, read only. Status is Placed, Not Enclosed or Redundant.

room.set sets a room's name and number from a script.

Labels and tags

Plans label each room with its name, number and area. Annotate › Room Tag (RT) adds a room tag, and Rooms can be scheduled with View › Schedules. See Tags and Schedules.

Rooms are saved as IfcSpace under their storey, with the number as the IFC Name and the name as its LongName.

Room separators

Architecture › Room & Area › Room Separator draws lines (or arcs, from the Shape row in Properties) that split space like walls without being built: an open-plan kitchen and living room become two rooms. Rooms on the level update at once. roomseparator.create

Colour schemes

Architecture › Room & Area › Color Schemes colours a plan's rooms (or an area plan's areas) by a parameter: Name, Number, Area, Perimeter, or any parameter of rooms, including your own (such as Department).

  • By value: each value gets a row. Values with no row of their own are coloured from a pastel palette, so a new department shows at once.
  • By range: rows by their At least value (m² for Area, m for Perimeter); a room falls in the row with the largest one not above its value.

Edit a row's colour (#rrggbb) or untick Visible to leave its rooms uncoloured. Show in this plan fills the active plan. colorscheme.create, colorscheme.set_rows, colorscheme.legend, view.set_color_scheme

Areas and area plans

Areas are measured by area schemes: Gross Building and Rentable to begin with, each with area types and rules for where boundaries sit on walls.

  1. Open a plan of the level. Architecture › Area › Area Plan: choose the scheme; with Boundaries on the walls round the outside, the outside walls are picked as boundaries. The area plan opens (Project Browser › Area Plans).
  2. Area Boundary picks the selected walls as more boundaries; they follow their walls, placed by the scheme's rules.
  3. Area: choose the area type, then click inside boundaries to place areas; Esc ends.

The rules place a picked wall's boundary by the areas either side: in Gross Building on the outside faces of the outside walls, so the area is the building's footprint; in Rentable on the centreline between two offices, and on the office's face next to an exterior area or the outside. Changing an area's type (area.set_type) moves its boundaries. Areas are IfcSpace (GFA for Gross Building) and boundaries IfcVirtualElement. areascheme.create, areascheme.set_rules, areaplan.create, areaboundary.create, area.place, area.list

Not yet available: other area rules, and volumes clipped by floors and roofs.

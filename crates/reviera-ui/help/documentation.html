Schedules

A schedule is a live table of one category's elements: doors, windows, rooms, walls, floors, ceilings or roofs. It updates as the model changes.

  1. View › Schedules, then choose the category. Schedules
  2. The schedule opens in its own tab, with one row per element and the totals of areas, volumes, lengths and counts at the bottom.
  3. Its Schedule tab on the ribbon has Fields… (choose the columns), Sort… (sort by a column), Itemize (switch between one row per element and one row per group of identical sorted values, with a count) and Export… (CSV or tab-separated text).

Editing a cell sets that parameter on the element (and can be undone); computed cells are grey and read-only. Selecting a row selects its elements. Schedules are listed under Schedules/Quantities in the Project Browser. schedule.create, schedule.set_fields, schedule.set_sort, schedule.set_cell, schedule.get_table, schedule.export

Sheets

  1. View › Sheet, then choose a title block size (ISO A0 to A4). Sheets are numbered A101, A102 and so on. Sheet
  2. With the sheet open, its Sheet tab has Place View: choose a plan, section or elevation, and it is placed at its scale with a numbered title. A view can be on one sheet only.
  3. Place Schedule adds a schedule's live table.
  4. Drag viewports and schedules to arrange them; Delete removes the selected one from the sheet. Open View opens the selected viewport's view.

The title block shows the project, the sheet's name and number, the date, the people and the scale (As indicated when the scales differ). With the sheet selected, Properties edit its values. sheet.create, sheet.set_parameter, sheet.place_view, sheet.place_schedule, viewport.move, viewport.remove

Tags

Tags show values read live from the element they tag. They are placed in plans, sections and elevations; in a section or elevation a tag hangs from its element as the view sees it (a door's or window's centre, a wall's middle).

Annotate › Tag by Category TGClick a door, window, room, wall or other element to tag it. Esc ends. Tag by Category
Room Tag RTClick inside a room: its name, number and area.
Tag All Not TaggedTick the categories: every element of them on the plan's level (in a section or elevation, that the view shows) that has no tag yet gets one.

Drag a tag to move it. In Properties, change its type, leader and orientation, or edit its value: that changes the element itself. Tags follow their elements; a tag whose element was deleted stays, drawn in orange. tag.create, tag.create_room, tag.tag_all, tag.set_type, tag.set_leader

Keynotes

Keynotes show keys of the project's keynote table (Manage › Settings › Load Keynotes). Annotate › Tag › Element Keynote: click an element, then where the keynote goes; it shows the keynote of the element's type (Edit Type › Keynote) and changes with it. User Keynote: choose a key from the table, click the element it is for, then where it goes. In Properties choose the style (the key in a box, the key alone, or its text) and the leader. A key the table does not have shows ? in orange. keynote.create, keynote.set, keynote.list

Keynote legends

View › Keynote Legend lists the keys the model's keynotes show, with their text, in a schedule tab. Answer Yes to Filter by sheet and each copy placed on a sheet (Sheet tab › Place Schedule) lists only the keynotes of the views on that sheet. Keynote Legend view.create_keynote_legend, view.set_keynote_legend

Legends

View › Legend makes a legend view at 1 : 50. In it, Annotate › Legend Component draws a type: choose a wall, floor, door, window or component type and how to look at it (plan, front or back elevation, or section across a wall or floor), then click where it goes. The drawing is of the type itself, so changing the type (its layers, its size) changes the legend. Add text with Text. Place the legend on as many sheets as you like. Legend view.create_legend, legend.place_component

Text

Annotate › Text (TX): click in a plan, section or elevation where the text's top-left corner goes, then type the text. Text types are 2.5, 3.5 and 5 mm high on paper. Properties edit a note's text, type, width (it wraps) and rotation; notes can have leaders. text.create, text.set, text.add_leader

Dimensions

Annotate › Aligned Dimension (DI) dimensions in plans, sections and elevations:

  1. Click a wall face, wall centreline, grid or cut edge; in a section or elevation also a level, a wall's base or top, or a wall's end.
  2. Click the next parallel reference; repeat for a string of dimensions.
  3. Click in empty space to place the dimension line through the cursor.

Dimensions to Reviera walls, grids, levels and reference planes follow them when they move. dim.create_aligned, anno.list

Spot elevations, coordinates and slopes

Annotate › Spot Elevation (EL) and Spot Coordinate work in plans, sections and elevations: click a point on an element, then where the text goes. A spot elevation reads the point's height; in a plan, the top of what is there (a slab's top, the top of a wall the plan cuts). A spot coordinate reads its north and east. Values are measured from the project base point, unless set to the survey point (shared coordinates, turned with true north) or, for elevations, a level. Top and Bottom shows the element's top and bottom where the spot is. Spots follow their element: raise a wall and its spot reads higher; a spot whose element has gone stays where it was, in orange. spot.create_elevation, spot.create_coordinate, spot.set, spot.list

Spot Slope: click a sloped face (a roof, a ramp). In a plan it shows the face's steepest slope with an arrow pointing downhill; in a section or elevation the slope of the profile the view shows, as an arrow or, set in Properties, a triangle. Properties choose percent, ratio (1 : n) or degrees. A vertical face has no slope and is refused. Like other spots, it follows its face when the model changes. spot.create_slope

Locks and EQ

A selected dimension's Properties have Lock Segment for each segment and, with two or more segments, EQ (also Modify | Dimensions › Lock and EQ):

  • A locked segment keeps its value: moving one of its walls, grids or reference planes moves the other with it. It reads with a padlock.
  • EQ makes the segments equal at once, by moving the references between the ends, and keeps them equal: move an outer wall and the inner ones follow. It reads "EQ".
  • An edit the constraints cannot follow (for example, when the element that would have to move is pinned) is refused with "Constraints are not satisfied"; nothing changes.
  • Constraints are kept when their dimension is deleted, and removed with their elements. View › Reveal Constraints shows them all in red; the selection's show dashed blue.

constraint.lock_dimension, constraint.unlock_dimension, constraint.set_equality, constraint.lock_alignment, constraint.delete, constraint.list, view.reveal_constraints

Temporary dimensions

With one wall, grid, reference plane, door or window selected in a plan, blue temporary dimensions show its distance to the nearest parallel wall, grid or reference plane on each side (a door or window: along its wall to the nearest crossing walls). Click a value and type a new one: the selected element moves, the other stays. dim.temporary_query, dim.temporary_set, dim.make_permanent

Export to PDF

Open a plan, ceiling plan, section, elevation or sheet, then File › Export PDF (Ctrl+P). The PDF is vector, at the view's scale, with pen widths in paper millimetres and real text. Export PDF… export.pdf

COBie handover

File › Export COBie writes a COBie 2.4 workbook (XLSX) with Contact, Facility, Floor, Space, Type, Component and Attribute sheets: every record with its IFC class and GlobalId, every component in its room, every parameter as an attribute. Missing required values are written as n/a, never invented. Reviera asks for the contact (company and e-mail address) when the model has none, and reports what is missing. Export COBie…

The export.cobie command can also write one CSV file per table; cobie.validate checks the data first and cobie.set_settings sets the contact.

Not yet available: crop regions, jogged sections, interior elevations, schedule filters and calculated fields, title block families and revisions, and tags or text in sections and on sheets.

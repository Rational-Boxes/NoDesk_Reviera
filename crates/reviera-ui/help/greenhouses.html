Greenhouses and glazed enclosures

A glazed enclosure is one element that lays out a whole greenhouse from a form and a few sizes: glass roofs, glass walls, knee walls and gutters, all ordinary Reviera elements. Change a size and its parts are made again.

See it in the Greenhouses sample (a Venlo range, a gutter-connected range and a hobby greenhouse), with its walkthrough.

Making one

  1. Open a plan of the level it stands on and click Architecture › Build › Glazed Enclosure Glazed Enclosure.
  2. Choose the form and type its sizes. Blank fields keep the form's usual values.
  3. Leave Place by clicking ticked and click its corner, then a point the length runs toward; or untick it to place it at the origin.

Forms

GableOne span with two roof slopes and glazed gable ends.
Gutter-connected multispanSeveral gable spans side by side, a valley gutter between each pair.
VenloA glass range of trusses, each carrying two (or more) narrow roofs. Type the truss width and the number of trusses.
SawtoothSpans of one roof slope each, rising from the gutter to a glazed vertical face at the high side. The top band of each face is vents, sized as a share of the span's floor (Face Vents), and the next span's gutter runs along its foot. Turn the faces away from the prevailing wind; it suits warm climates.
Hoop tunnelA film tunnel: a semicircular arch across the span on sidewalls of the eave height (0 for ground to ground), hoops at most 1.5 m apart.
Gothic tunnelAs the hoop, but two arcs (each of radius 0.6 × the span) meet in a pointed crown, which sheds condensation and snow and gives more height near the sides.

The sizes: the length along the ridges, the span (or truss width), the number of spans (or trusses), the bay spacing, the eave (gutter) height, the roof pitch, and an optional knee wall under the glass. The usual values are provisional and listed for expert review.

If the roof is flatter than its glazing may be laid at (2.5° for glass systems, 5° for polycarbonate and film), or under 10°, where condensate drips onto the crop instead of running to the gutters, Properties shows a Warnings row.

What it is made of

  • Roofs: one sloped-glazing roof per span, of Greenhouse Roof Glass (1,125 mm panes on 40 × 80 mm bars).
  • Walls: curtain walls of Greenhouse Wall Glass (800 mm panes). The end walls are attached to every roof, so their glass rises into the gables; any curtain wall can be attached to roofs this way with Attach Top (wall.attach).
  • Frame: square steel posts at every bay on each gutter line, inside the glass, and a gutter beam of the same tube along each line of posts, carrying the gutter; structural columns and beams, so the structural tools see them. Untick Frame in Properties for none.
  • Knee walls when a height is set, along the sides.
  • Doors: one in the middle of each end wall, made of the panes there merged into a curtain-wall door at least 1.5 m wide and 2.1 m high. Where there is a knee wall the end walls are glazed to the ground, so the doors stand on the floor. Untick Doors in Properties for none.
  • Gutters on the outer eaves and between the spans, laid level.
  • Ridge vents: panes of each roof's top row become vents, hung from the ridge and drawn open, alternating side to side along it, until their area reaches the share of the floor set in Properties › Ridge Vents (18 % unless changed; 0 for none). Properties shows the vent area they give.

Select the enclosure, or any of its parts, and Properties › Glazed Enclosure shows its form and sizes, its ridge height and floor area. Editing a size makes the parts again, so edits made to a part by hand are lost; enclosure.detach keeps a part as it is.

Glazing

Properties › Roof Glazing and Wall Glazing choose the glazing from Reviera's library: horticultural float, toughened, diffuse and laminated glass, a low-e double unit, twin- and five-wall polycarbonate, multi-skin acrylic, and single or inflated double polyethylene film. Each glazing type carries its light and PAR transmission, haze, U-value, g-value, safety class and weight (the Reviera_GlazingUnit property set, published so other tools can read it), and the energy model takes its U-value. The values are typical ones; set the specified product's with glazing.set_type. glazing.list_types, glazing.create_type

Properties › Frame System chooses the glazing bars: aluminium 40 × 80 mm (the default for glass) or 50 × 100 mm, galvanised steel hoops (the tunnels' default), timber 50 × 100 mm, or uPVC 60 × 90 mm. The glazing types are made with that frame's bars and carry its material, finish and the deflection limit of members carrying glass (L/175, at most 15 mm; the Reviera_FrameSystem property set). Frame Shading shows the share of the roof the bars shade. frame.list_types, frame.create_type, frame.set_type

Headhouse

Properties › Headhouse adds a solid-walled workroom across one end of the range (potting, packing, boiler): walls on its three outer sides, a flat roof and a door in its front wall, and the range's end-wall door into it widened to 3 m for trolleys. Its Depth starts at 10 % of the growing floor (at least 4 m), its Height at the eaves; At puts it at the start or the end of the range. enclosure.set with headhouse

Vents

Ridge vents (Properties › Ridge Vents, a share of the floor area) and Side Vents (a band along each side wall's top) are panes that open, hung from their upper edge. Vent Opening Angle and Vent Actuator set how they open; Free / Effective Vent Area shows the opening at that angle and the opening times its discharge coefficient (0.6), which is what airflow sees. Roll-up Sides open the side walls (a tunnel's sidewalls too) from the ground up to that height, the film drawn rolled up at the top; Gable Vents put a band of vents that deep under the top of each end wall, above the doors. Each vent window carries its values in IFC (Reviera_Vent). enclosure.set_vents

Geodesic domes

Architecture › Geodesic Dome: choose the frequency (2V to 6V: more, shorter struts and a rounder dome) and the truncation (the share of the sphere kept: 1/2 a hemisphere, 5/8 taller), then click the centre and a point at the sphere's radius in a plan. The dome is triangular glass panels on struts, cut at the ring of hubs nearest the truncation; some frequencies and cuts give a level base and others a base that steps between rings, which Properties says. Properties › Geodesic Dome changes its radius, frequency, truncation, glazing and frame, and lists its hubs, panels and struts, with the struts by length class (A, B, C, shortest first) for fabrication. roof.create_geodesic, roof.set_geodesic, roof.geodesic

Growing layouts

Select a greenhouse (or a room, for an indoor grow room) and click Architecture › Build › Growing Layout Growing Layout. Choose the system and how it runs, and the floor is filled, clear of the walls and the posts:

Benches1.8 m wide with 0.6 m aisles.
Rolling benchesIn groups of five, 50 mm apart, with one aisle per group, opened by rolling them: more of the floor grows.
Ebb-and-flow tablesTrays that flood from below.
NFT tablesChannels 100 mm wide at 200 mm centres, falling 2 %, no run longer than 12 m.
High-wire guttersFor tomatoes and cucumbers: hanging substrate gutters 250 mm wide, one per row 1.25 m apart, falling 0.5 % to their drain ends, under crop wires at 3.25 m (Properties › Crop Wire; kept under the eaves or the room's top).

In a room the rows run along its longest wall (Along) or across it, each cut to the room's outline, clear of its walls; after changing the room, change a layout size to lay it out again.

Across runs them from the side walls to a main aisle along the middle, in line with the doors; Along runs them the length of the house, split by a main aisle across the middle. Each bench or table is its own element (category Growing Systems), so schedules count them. Select the layout or any piece and Properties › Growing Layout shows its growing area and its share of the floor; change a size there, or the greenhouse's, and it is laid out again. growing.layout, growing.set_layout, growing.summary

Growing climate and crop light

Select a greenhouse and, in Properties › Growing Climate, choose the Crop: tomato, cucumber, sweet pepper, head lettuce, strawberry, bedding and potted plants, or propagation. Each brings its target daily light integral (DLI, mol/m²·d), day and night temperatures, CO₂ set point and photoperiod (the longest day it takes under lamps), from public extension sources; change any of them there. None removes the climate. growing.crops, enclosure.set_climate

The rows under it show the crop's light month by month (enclosure.crop_light):

  • the outdoor light, from the weather file (Sustainability › Site › Weather; import it again if it was imported before this release) or, without one, an estimate from the project's latitude (Manage › Project Location) with a clearness index of 0.5;
  • through the roof: × the roof glazing's PAR transmission and the share its bars leave open (Roof Transmission);
  • the Darkest Month's natural light and what the lamps add, the Lamp PPFD it needs over the photoperiod, and the Months Short of Target.

Grow lights. With the greenhouse selected, Architecture › Build › Grow Lights Grow Lights hangs LED top-lights under the gutters on a grid across each span, enough for the darkest month (110 µmol/m²·s without a climate). Each fixture is a light fixture element (1,500 µmol/s at 600 W unless changed); select one and Properties › Grow Lights changes the photon flux, power, target PPFD, height and maintenance factor, and shows the count, mean PPFD, the DLI the lamps add, the load in kW and W/m², and a warning for fixtures below 2.3 µmol/J. They are laid out again when the greenhouse changes. growing.lights, growing.set_lights, growing.lights_summary

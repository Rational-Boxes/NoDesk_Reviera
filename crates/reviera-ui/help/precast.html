Precast

Walls and floors split into precast pieces by rules you set once per project, on the Concrete tab. Each piece is an assembly with its piece mark.

See it in the Reinforced and precast concrete sample (a wall and a slab split into pieces, with bars, fabric, anchors and shop drawings), with its walkthrough.

Configuration

Precast Settings › Configuration holds the rules as a tree:

  • General: the concrete density that gives weights (2.5 t/m³ by default), automatic updates, and the data folder.
  • Solid Wall and Double Wall › Segmentation: minimum and maximum length and height, maximum weight, the vertical and horizontal joint gaps, the minimum distance from a joint to an opening, and the profile overlap height.
  • Solid, Hollow-core and Lattice-girder Slab › Segmentation: minimum and maximum width, maximum weight and joint gap; for hollow-core also the slab component, division profile and strand diameter.
  • CAM Export: format, folder and filename rule ({mark}, {project}, {source}, {index}).

A value that breaks a rule turns red, its page is marked (!), the message below names the rule, and OK stays disabled until it is fixed. Each OK raises the configuration's version. The dialog says how many assemblies an older version made and can re-split them. Export… and Import… share the rules between projects as a JSON file. The defaults are provisional starting points, not values from a standard, and are awaiting review by a subject-matter expert.

The Shop Drawing pages, Annotations and Dimensioning are for the later precast steps. They are not built yet.

Split

Select straight walls or floors and click Split. Choose the wall kind (solid or double) or the slab kind (solid, hollow-core or lattice-girder).

  • A wall makes the fewest pieces within the maximum length and weight. Rows of equal height are added when it is taller than the maximum height. No joint comes nearer an opening than the minimum distance, and pieces are as even as that allows.
  • A floor is cut into strips along its longest edge. Hollow-core slabs take standard widths with the rest in the last piece.
  • Joints are the configured gap wide. Weights are volume × density.

The element's parts are divided (they are made first if it has none). Each piece's parts go in an assembly marked W1-1, W1-2, … (S for slabs; a letter for the row when there are rows). In the Model Tree the assemblies sit under the element. In IFC each is an IfcElementAssembly (PRECAST_ASSEMBLY) holding its parts, with Pset_PrecastConcreteElementFabrication PieceMark and TypeDesignator. Re-split splits again with the current rules. Splitting an element twice asks to re-split instead.

Auto Reinforce

Select precast assemblies (or their pieces, or the elements split) of one kind and click Auto Reinforce. Only the reinforcement types whose thickness range holds the pieces are offered.

  • Area reinforcement: vertical and horizontal bars at both faces of a wall piece, or bars both ways at the top and bottom of a slab piece. They stay inside the piece less the edge cover and are cut round openings with the cover.
  • Edge U-bars (walls): round every free edge of the piece and every side, head and sill of an opening. The web is across the thickness and the legs run into the piece.

The bars' partition is the piece mark, so each piece is numbered on its own, and the piece's assembly holds them in IFC. Running again replaces what the last run made and keeps sets you edited since, and bars you added in the piece's partition. A report lists what was made, replaced, kept and skipped. The types are edited in Configuration › Reinforcement, one per line: id; from-to (mm); bar type; spacing (mm); [leg (mm);] name. The starting types are provisional and are awaiting review by a subject-matter expert.

Custom fabric sheets

Select precast assemblies and click Custom Fabric, then choose a rule. Each piece gets welded sheets shaped to it, at the rule's face or faces:

  • The main wires run along the piece's longer side.
  • Wires stop at the rule's clearance round openings and at its edge distance from the piece's outer edges and from its joints with other pieces. Wires shorter than the shortest allowed are left out and counted.
  • A piece larger than the largest sheet gets several sheets, lapped by the rule's lap.

Sheets are numbered like other fabric, so identical pieces share sheet numbers. Their partition is the piece mark, and the piece's assembly holds them. Running again replaces sheets as they were made and keeps sheets you edited. Rules are edited in Configuration › Custom Fabric Sheet Rules, one per line. The starting rule is provisional and is awaiting review by a subject-matter expert.

Mounting parts

Select precast assemblies and click Mounting Parts.

  • Each piece is weighed from its volume (openings removed) and the density. The weight band that holds it gives the number of lifting anchors. A piece heavier than the last band gets none and is listed.
  • The anchors go on a wall piece's top edge, or a slab piece's top face, symmetric about the piece's centre of gravity. They keep the configured distance from the ends and from openings. When no symmetric places do, they go to the nearest valid places and you are warned.
  • Anchors move like other elements. Running again replaces anchors as placed and keeps those you moved, and anchors you placed by hand.
  • The piece's assembly holds them, and gets its weight as NetWeight in IFC.

The weight bands and distances are edited in Configuration › (kind) › Connections. The starting rules are provisional and are awaiting review by a subject-matter expert.

Shop drawings

Select precast assemblies and click Shop Drawings. Each piece gets an A3 sheet, or each group of identical pieces shares one. Identical means the same outline, openings, reinforcement, sheets and anchors to the millimetre. The sheet shows:

  • the front view at a standard scale, with chain dimensions of the ends, openings and anchors, the height, and each opening's size;
  • the centre-of-gravity symbol with its distances;
  • a section through the thickness;
  • a bill of materials: concrete, rebar by number, fabric sheets and mounting parts;
  • the piece marks it stands for.

The drawing is worked out from the pieces each time the sheet is shown or printed, so it follows changes. Making shop drawings again replaces the earlier sheets for those pieces.

CAM export

Select precast assemblies (or none, for all) and click CAM Export. Choose a writer and a folder.

  • The built-in writers give each piece as a neutral JSON or CSV file: its outline and openings, lifting anchors, every bar with its shape, fabric wires and marks. Coordinates are in millimetres from the lower-left corner of the piece's front face.
  • Other writers are programs installed with a manifest in the data folder's cam_writers. They are given the neutral JSON.
  • Files are named by the filename rule, for example {Name:12:4:0:left}_{ProdNo}, from the variables Name, ProdNo, Date, Time and Mark. A mistake in the rule is reported with its position before anything is written. Names that would collide get a number.

The export does not change the model. Pieces without reinforcement or mounting parts are listed as warnings.

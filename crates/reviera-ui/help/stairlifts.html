Stairlifts and platform stairlifts

A stairlift runs on a rail along one side of a stair: a seated stairlift, or a wheelchair platform lift (an inclined platform lift). Reviera sizes it from the vertical-transport rule packs in use, makes it as one element following the stair, and checks what it leaves of the stair. The sample Stairlifts and platform stairlifts in a parish hall and its house shows the tools.

Placing one

Select a stair and choose Architecture › Circulation › Stairlift. Choose a seated lift or a wheelchair platform lift, the side it runs on going up (usually the wall), and what the stair serves: a dwelling, an exit enclosure, or anything else with the number of people. For a platform lift choose a platform size, or leave the family's largest. Parks clear of the stair is for a rail end that hinges or slides away, or runs round a corner.

How it is sized

  • Seated: makers' generic sizes, folding to 320 mm from the wall and 530 mm in use, its rail standing 160 mm in. It suits stairs of 25–50° and straight rails up to 7.06 m.
  • Platform, straight rails: 800 × 1250 down to 675 × 1000 mm platforms, 250 kg, folding to 260–299 mm. A stair that turns takes the curved family instead: tubes that follow the landings, 800 × 1220 down to 700 × 750 mm, 300 kg, with a drive box at the top.
  • How far the platform reaches past the bottom nosing, and how high its deck rises above the nosings, come from the makers' tables for the stair's angle. Wall loads at a support are recorded for the structure (Reviera_Stairlift).

The rail runs along the flights and landings, down to the floor before the first riser and on past the top nosing. The unit is shown parked at the foot of the stair.

The width the stair keeps

Under the IBC a lift on a dwelling's stair (R-3, or within an R-2 unit) leaves at least 20 in (508 mm) beside the folded seat or platform; any other stair keeps its required width (44 in, or 36 in serving fewer than 50 people). In England the escape stair keeps its width with the unit parked (800 mm for up to 50 people), and the rail itself is ignored. A unit that parks clear of the stair leaves only its rail beside it.

Running envelope, head box and landings

Each lift has virtual volumes: its running envelope over each straight flight, as deep as the unit in use and as high as a seated rider or, for a platform, its deck plus the 1524 mm head box A18.1 asks; and a landing zone at each end, with a wheelchair space beyond a platform's (30 × 48 in under ADA, 1500 × 1500 mm in England). View › Coordination › Check Clearances lists anything in them, such as the floor above a stair whose opening was cut for walking headroom only.

Check Stairlifts

Architecture › Circulation › Check Stairlifts checks the width left beside the unit, a unit in an exit enclosure (NFPA 101's reading: a note for the authority), the platform's size (England asks 800 × 1250 mm) and speed (0.15 m/s), and the generic model's limits: the stair's angle, the longest straight rail, the makers' recommended stair width, and the width a turn needs on its inside. The makers' limits are notes.

Limits and what to check

The lift is made for its stair as it is: if the stair changes, delete the lift and place it again (the checks and zones follow the stair as it is now). Not yet built: running envelopes over winders, spirals and the landings of a turning stair; the head box over a curved rail's turns; the backrest and footrest clearances; the charger outlet or dedicated circuit; and outdoor units.

Scripts and agents

Everything above is a command: stairlift.create, stairlift.list, stairlift.delete and stairlift.check; clearance.list and clearance.check include the zones. Lua scripts and AI agents (MCP, the modeling toolset) can use them.

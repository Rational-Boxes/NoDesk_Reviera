Structural framing

Structural columns, beams, braces, beam systems and trusses on the Structure tab. Each has a type that carries its section (from a standard catalogue or your own), its structural material and its designation. The samples Steel-framed office, Mass timber frame and Wood-frame house show them together.

Sections

Structure › Load Section Load Section (columns), Load Beam Section and Load Brace Section make a type from a standard section: AISC W, C, L, WT, HSS and pipe; EN IPE, HEA and HEB; UK UB and UC; North American dimension lumber (PS 20 dressed sizes) and European sawn and glued-laminated timber. The catalogues usual for the model's building code are starred. The type takes the region's usual grade (ASTM A992 steel, S355, 350W …) unless you choose another material.

Structural columns

  • Column: click in a plan; the column runs from the plan's level to the next. At Grids puts one at every crossing of the chosen grids.
  • Slanted Column: click its foot, then its head on the level above (or give an angle).
  • Attach Top and Detach Top join a column's top to the beam, floor or roof above it.
  • Timber columns are checked for slenderness (le/d ≤ 50, NDS and CSA O86): column.check.

Beams

Beam (B M): click its start and end in a plan, at the plan's level (an offset at each end slopes it). Beams join columns, walls and other beams where they meet and are cut back there; their usage (girder, joist, purlin …) follows from what they rest on unless you lock it. On Grids runs beams along chosen grid lines between the columns. Properties: Start/End Level Offset (set them to the slab's depth, negative, so the slab sits on the beams), y and z justification, rotation, cutback and usage.

Braces

Brace (B R): click its two ends in a plan; it runs from this level to the next, each end at its own offset, attached to the columns or beams it meets. Draw two crossing braces for X-bracing.

Beam systems

Beam System: click inside a bay bounded by beams or walls: infill beams are laid across it, along the bay's first edge or a chosen direction, by a fixed distance, a fixed number, a maximum spacing or a clear spacing, justified to the start, end, centre or the edges. Changing the bay moves the beams. Remove Beam System keeps them as plain beams. Scripts draw a system's boundary themselves (beam_system.create_sketch).

Trusses

Truss (T S): click its start and end; the truss's bottom chord (or top, with Bearing Chord) lies on that line at the level. Select one of its members to set, in Properties:

LayoutPratt, Howe, Warren, Fink (the W of a pitched roof truss), King post or Queen post.
ProfileFlat, Gable (rising to the height at mid-span), Mono-pitch, Scissor (the bottom chord rises too) or Bowstring (curved).
Panels, Truss Height, Heel HeightA pitched truss with a heel has posts at its bearings. Stretching a truss keeps its panels.

Chords are split where they bend. Scripts can give any chord shape (truss.edit_profile) and make the top chord follow a roof's underside (truss.attach, truss.detach). Remove Truss keeps the members as plain framing.

Structural walls and floors

Wall draws bearing walls; Properties' Structural Usage sets bearing, shear, structural combined or non-bearing. Floor sketches a structural floor; its span direction is the first edge drawn, or any edge (floor.set_span_direction). Analysis carries a floor's area loads across its span.

Connections

Select the members meeting at a joint and click Connection, then choose a type that fits the members:

  • Shear Plate: a column (or girder) then a beam; a single plate welded to the main member and bolted through the beam's web, its height following the bolt rows.
  • Extended End Plate: a column then a beam; a plate welded across the beam's end, bolted to the column's flange in pairs above and below the beam.
  • Base Plate: one or more columns, each getting its own; a plate under the column with four anchor rods into the footing, welded all round.
  • Gusset: a column or beam then a brace; a plate on the brace's line, bolted through it. At a column's foot it stops at the column's base, above the base plate.
  • Generic: any two or more members; a symbol in plans with no parts.

Every connection keeps its approval status and fabricator. Its plates, bolts and welds are elements of their own, shown only in views at Detail Level Fine (3D views have the row in their Properties) and kept in step with the members: move a beam and its plates follow; delete the connection and they go with it.

Select a connection, or one of its plates, bolts or welds, and Properties shows its Connection Type (change it to give the connection another type) and the type's parameters by tab: plates, bolts, welds and layout. Changing a parameter changes it for this connection only, its row marked ●. To change a value for every connection of the type, type key=value into Type Parameters… (for example bolt_diameter=7/8"); the row's help names the keys. The defaults follow the region: inch sizes under US codes, millimetres elsewhere.

Automating connections

Automate Connections places library connections on every join in the model at once, or on the joins of the selected members: base plates under columns (where no column stands below), shear plates where beams meet columns or girders, and gussets at braces' ends. Choose:

  • Rule set: Default, or one of the project's. A rule set is an ordered list of rules; a join gets the type of the first rule that matches its kind, its members' sections and the connected member's depth. A rule without a depth range uses the type's range: shear plates for beams 150–920 mm deep, end plates 200–920 mm. Scripts save rule sets with structural_connection.set_rule_set.
  • Joins that already have a connection: skip them, or give generic connections the matched type. A detailed connection is never changed.
  • Preview first: each join is marked in 3D views, coloured by the type it would get (blue base plate, green shear plate, purple end plate, orange gusset, grey skipped), and the log lists every join with its status and reason. Apply places them as previewed; nothing changes before.

Only steel members are connected. Where a type cannot be made, a generic connection marked Failed holds the join. The run is one undo step, kept in the project's run history; the log saves as CSV.

Plates, bolts and welds

Structure › Steel details steelwork by hand. Everything placed here is drawn only in views at Detail Level Fine.

  • Plate: select one column, beam, brace or plate and choose its face (flange 1 or 2, web 1 or 2, start or end; a plate's top or bottom), or select nothing to use the view's work plane (a plan's level). Click the contour's corners; Enter, or a click on the first corner, finishes. The plate is 10 mm thick and sits on the face's outside (justification Bottom); change its thickness, justification, role and material in Properties. A plate on a face moves with its element, and stays where it was if the element is deleted.
  • Bolts: select the elements to join (one for shear studs). Choose bolts, anchors, holes or shear studs, the face the fasteners are square to, and a rectangular or circular layout, then click where the pattern's centre goes. Reviera finds the grip through the elements (an I-section's far flange is not counted), the bolt length (grip, two washers, a nut and thread, rounded up) and the holes in each element, shown in Properties.
  • Weld: select the elements to weld and, in a 3D view, the edges where they touch are drawn; the one under the cursor turns red. Click it, then choose the weld type, throat and shop or site. A weld keeps its place when the elements change and is marked needs review in Properties until you accept it there. It goes when one of its elements is deleted.

In IFC, plates are IfcPlate, bolt patterns IfcElementAssembly (FASTENER_GROUP) aggregating IfcMechanicalFastener bolts, anchors and studs, holes IfcOpeningElements voiding their elements, and welds IfcFastener with Pset_FastenerWeld.

Cuts and modifiers

The small Steel buttons cut members and plates. Cuts are parametric: they follow the members they are made from, and Edit › Undo takes each back in one step.

  • Cope: select two members that meet. The one whose end is at the joint loses what lies in the other's flange zones, plus a 13 mm (1/2") clearance; make the girder deeper and the cope follows. Uncope removes the copes on the selected members.
  • Miter: two members meeting at their ends are cut on the plane halving the corner.
  • Saw Cut: the first selected member's end is cut flush with the second's flange or web.
  • Cut Through and Cut By: the first selected element loses the second's shape (with a gap, for Cut Through).
  • Shorten: a beam's or brace's end moves back (a negative length extends it), with angles for a diagonal end.
  • Corner Cut: a plate's corner is cut straight, rounded or notched.
  • Contour Cut: sketch a hole or notch on a face of the selected element, as for a plate.

In IFC each cut is an IfcOpeningElement voiding its element, named by its kind (COPE, MITER, …); the cuts made together form a sub-connection.

Custom connections

Select the connections at a join and any plates, bolts and welds you added there, then Customize (Steel › Custom) and name the new connection type. It can join at most three members. The selection becomes its first instance. Connection then offers the type for joins with as many members, and places it where the members have the same roles and sections and meet the same way (a beam meeting a column's web is not one meeting its flange): the connections are made for the new members and the plates, bolts and welds where the type puts them.

Add to Custom adds loose plates, bolts or welds placed at an instance to its type, and Remove from Custom removes selected parts; both change every instance, after asking. Custom Parameter makes a part's thickness, bolt diameter, weld throat or connection parameter a type parameter and sets it for every instance.

Checking connections

Select a connection (or one of its plates or bolts) and type its design forces in Properties, per load case or combination: ULS: 0, 200, 0, 0 is a group named ULS with N 0, V 200, Vh 0 and M 0 (kN and kN·m; tension positive; V along the connected member's depth, as a beam's end reaction). Separate groups with ;.

Check (Steel › Connections) asks a connection design provider and design code, then checks the selected connections: Properties show the status, the largest utilisation and each check's ratio. Presize makes failing connections bigger step by step (bolt size and count, plate thickness, weld size) until they pass, in one undo step. Check Report writes an HTML report. A check goes back to not calculated when the connection, its members or its forces change.

The sample provider checks bolt shear, bearing and tear-out, bolt tension and fillet welds to AISC 360 or EN 1993-1-8 in a simplified way (no eccentricity, prying or block shear): it shows how providers work and is not a complete connection design. Providers are Lua plugins with capability = "connection_check" and a check(input) function.

Numbering and NC files

Numbering (Steel › Fabrication) gives every steel part a part mark and every shop assembly an assembly mark, and lists them. Identical parts share a mark however they are placed: a member by its section, length, holes and cuts; a plate by its thickness, outline and holes. Marks are c, b, br and p and a number. An assembly is a member and everything shop-welded to it, its own shop welds and its connections' welded plates (a base plate with its column, a shear plate with the column it is welded to); site welds and bolts do not join. Its mark is the prefix in capitals. Each element's marks are in Properties and its part mark is its IFC Tag. Number again after changing the model.

NC Export writes one DSTV NC1 file per part mark (b1.nc1, …) to a folder, for saws, drills and cutting machines: a member's profile, length and holes on its web and flanges, a plate's outline and holes, and how many to make.

Coarse views

Set a plan's Detail Level to Coarse and beams, braces and truss members are drawn as single lines along their location lines, braces with their symbol, cut back at joints by the distance in Structural Settings.

In IFC

Columns are IfcColumn (LoadBearing true), beams IfcBeam, braces IfcMember (BRACE); beam systems and trusses are IfcElementAssembly aggregating their members (truss chords CHORD, webs STRUT); sections are IfcProfileDefs on the types. A connection's plates are IfcPlate, its bolts and anchor rods IfcMechanicalFastener (BOLT, ANCHORBOLT, with their nominal diameter and length) and its welds IfcFastener (WELD), all aggregated by the connection.

Not yet available: the beam join editor, arc beams, placing braces in elevations, truss families, moment and bracing connections beyond the four library types, connection code checking, cope and cut detailing and rebar.

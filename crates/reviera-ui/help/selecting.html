Selecting and modifying

Select elements in a view, then change them in Properties, with grips, or with the tools of the Modify tab. Every change can be undone.

Selecting

ClickSelects the element under the cursor. Clicking empty space, or Esc, clears the selection.
Ctrl+clickAdds the element to the selection.
Shift+clickRemoves it from the selection.
Modify MDEnds the running tool, so clicks select again.
TabCycles what is under the cursor: the element, then the chain of walls joined to it, then other elements there (Shift+Tab goes back). The status bar names it; the next click selects it.

The selection decides what Properties show and which contextual tab appears. The Model Tree, schedules (selecting a row) and Check Overlaps also select elements. In plans, a room is selected by clicking its label or inside it. selection.get, selection.set, selection.add, selection.remove, selection.clear

Box selection

Drag a box over the elements:

  • Left to right draws a solid window: it selects what is entirely inside.
  • Right to left draws a dashed crossing: it selects what is inside or touches it.

In plans, a left drag that starts on nothing draws the box. In 3D views a left drag orbits, so hold Ctrl or Shift while dragging. Ctrl adds the boxed elements to the selection and Shift removes them. Only what the view shows can be boxed: its view range, section box, slices and hidden elements are respected.

The quick modify menu

A middle click (press and release without moving; a middle drag pans) on an element opens a menu at the cursor for the selection. The element is selected first if it is not already (Ctrl keeps the rest of the selection). It offers:

  • Move, Copy, Rotate, Mirror – Pick Axis, Mirror – Draw Axis;
  • Flip Wall for walls, Flip Hand and Flip Facing for doors and windows;
  • Hide in View, Make Translucent / Make Opaque, Isolate, Reset Hide/Isolate (when anything is hidden) and Section Box;
  • Pin or Unpin, and Delete.

With exactly two elements selected, the menu checks whether they overlap. When they do, it offers the booleans: cut either by the other, intersect, union, join (by precedence), or accept the overlap. See Booleans.

Modify tools

The tools are on the Modify tab, on every contextual tab and in the Modify menu. They work in plan views. Move, Copy, Rotate and Mirror act on the selection made first (select, then start the tool). Points snap like the drawing tools, and lengths and angles can be typed and placed with Enter. Esc drops the last pick, then ends the tool. Align, Offset, Trim/Extend and Split pick elements in the view and stay active for more picks.

Measuring

Modify › Measure Measure measures in a plan without changing anything. Click points: the status bar shows each segment, its angle and the running total. Esc clears the chain; a second Esc ends the tool.

Scripts measure with measure.between (the distance between two points, its components and its angle), measure.chain (a chain's segments and total) and measure.along (the length of a wall, curtain wall, ramp or grid along its curve).

Grips and dragging

Many elements can be changed by dragging, each drag one undo step:

Walls and gridsA selected wall or grid shows a grip at each end: drag it to move that end. Drag the selected walls or grids themselves to move them.
Doors and windowsDrag a selected one along its wall.
Floors, ceilings, roofs, decksIn a plan, a selected one shows grips at its corners and edge middles: drag an edge across, or a corner. See Sketch mode.
Vertical openings and shaftsThe same corner and edge grips. See Openings.
Section box and slicesFace grips and slice grips in 3D views.
Tags, text, viewportsDrag them in plans and on sheets.

Deleting and pinning

Delete (DE, or the Delete key) deletes the selection along with what depends on it: a wall's doors and windows, a dormer's parts. Deleting a level first lists what would go with it. element.delete

Pin (PN) keeps the selected elements from being moved or deleted; Unpin (UP) releases them. element.pin, element.unpin

Undo, redo and history

Edit › Undo (Ctrl+Z) and Redo (Ctrl+Y). Every change is one transaction; a tool that makes several changes, and a whole script run, undo as one step. The history records who made each change (you, a script or an agent); the edit.history command lists it, and edit.undo takes a number of steps.

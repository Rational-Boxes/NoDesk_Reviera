MEP connectors and systems

Mechanical, plumbing and electrical components carry connectors: the points where ducts, pipes and cables join them. Connected components form networks, and terminals, fixtures and equipment are grouped into systems such as Supply Air 1 or Domestic Cold Water 1. The tools are on the Systems tab.

Components with connectors

Load MEP Types adds Reviera's starter types: Air Handling Unit, Supply Diffuser, Return Grille, Exhaust Fan, Water Heater, Circulation Pump, Lavatory, Water Closet, Distribution Board, Receptacle and Ceiling Luminaire. They are simple boxes with typical connectors, for laying out systems before you have manufacturers' content; their sizes and flows are placeholders to change. MEP Component places any type that has connectors, like Place Component.

A connector has a domain (Duct, Pipe, Electrical, Cable Tray or Conduit), a point and an outward direction, a shape and size, and for ducts and pipes a system classification (Supply Air, Return Air, Exhaust Air, Domestic Cold Water, Sanitary, Hydronic Supply, …), a flow direction (In, Out or Bidirectional) and a flow. Electrical connectors carry the voltage, poles, load and power factor. Scripts and agents add connectors to any component type with connector.add; a placed copy can have its own flow (connector.set).

When elements with connectors are selected, each connector is drawn as a ring with a stalk pointing out: blue for ducts, green for pipes, orange for electrical, and red while it is open.

Ducts and pipes

Duct (DT) and Pipe (PL) ask for the type, shape and size, the middle elevation (the centreline's height above the level), the system type and, for pipes, a slope. Then click the run's points in a plan:

  • an elbow goes at every corner;
  • press E to change the elevation: the run rises or drops at the last point, with an elbow at each end of the riser;
  • end (or start) the run on the side of another duct or pipe, square to it, and a tee or a tap joins them;
  • Enter or Esc makes the run; the tool stays for the next one.

A run that meets a connector connects to it, and joins that connector's system when it carries the same system type. Pipes drawn with a slope fall along their level legs from the first point. Cap Open Ends closes the selection's open ends.

Which elbow, tee, tap or cap is used comes from the type's routing preferences: for each role a list of parts with a size range, tried from the top. Where none fits, nothing is inserted, the end is left open and a warning says so. routing_preferences.set changes them and mep_run.reapply_type refits existing runs. The fittings are Reviera's own compact mitred parts; their proportions, like the starter types' sizes and flows, are provisional values awaiting review by an engineer.

Connections

Placing or moving a component connects its open connectors that meet another's: in the same place, facing each other, of the same domain, shape and size. Connect does it for the selection. mep.connect and mep.disconnect join and separate two connectors by hand; a duct connector never joins a pipe connector, and different sizes are refused until fittings with transitions exist.

Systems

Select terminals, fixtures or equipment and choose Create System. Pick the system type (the list offers the classifications of the selection's connectors) and a name, or leave the name empty for the next free one. Each element joins by its connectors of that classification; a supply diffuser cannot join a return air system, and a connector is in one system at a time.

  • Select Equipment: select the air handling unit, pump or water heater that serves a system, then choose the system.
  • Add To System and Remove From System change the members.
  • Divide System splits a system whose members form separate networks into Name 1, Name 2, and so on.
  • Disconnect Equipment stops the equipment serving the system.

Electrical circuits

Select receptacles, luminaires or other devices and choose Create Circuit, then the kind (Power, Data, Fire Alarm, …). Devices join by their electrical connector of that kind; for power, those of another voltage or number of poles are skipped and listed. A device can be on one circuit at a time: adding it to another moves it.

A panel is a distribution board. Panel Settings gives it a name, a distribution system (230/400 V wye, 120/208 V wye, …), its number of slots and how its circuits are named. Select Panel lists only the panels that give the circuit's voltage and have free slots, and puts the circuit in the first free ones: a two- or three-pole circuit takes every second slot. Circuits are numbered by the scheme: Panel Name + Number (LP1-7), Numerical (13,15,17) or Prefixed (C7). Disconnect Panel takes it off again; it keeps its devices.

Select a device or a panel to see its circuits drawn dashed from the panel to each device. The System Browser lists panels, their circuits with the load, and their devices. Check Circuits marks circuits with no panel or on a panel that does not fit them.

Cable tray and conduit

Cable Tray (CT) and Conduit (CN) draw runs like ducts: set the size and height, click the points, and press E to rise or drop. Corners get bends with a radius: for tray, its width times the type's multiplier; for conduit, the radius its standard gives for the diameter (otherwise the diameter times the multiplier). You can type another radius in the form. Conduit sizes come from the type's standard, IEC 61386 (16 to 63 mm) or EMT (1/2" to 2"); a size the standard does not list is refused. A type made without fittings draws its bends as curved lengths of tray or conduit instead.

Cable Tray Fitting and Conduit Fitting ask for the part (tee, cross, elbow, coupling, cap, junction box, or Split) and then place it where you click: on an open end, or along a run, which is split around it and stays connected. Types without fittings refuse them. Upgrade Fitting (+) turns the selected elbow into a tee and a tee into a cross; Downgrade Fitting (−) removes an open leg. Parallel Conduits copies the selected conduit run side by side and in rows. Concentric bends share their centre, so the outer conduits bend wider; this works for conduit types without fittings.

Ducts, pipes, trays and conduit above the plan's cut height are drawn in plans as seen from above, up to the top of the view range.

Feeders and transformers

Select a panel or a transformer and choose Select Supply. The list offers only the panels that can feed it: their distribution system must match (a transformer gives its secondary system, set in Panel Settings), they need free slots, and the choice must not make a loop. A feeder takes slots on a breaker, or none on the source's feed-through lugs (turned on in Panel Settings; one feeder per panel). The fed panel's whole load then counts on its source. Panel Hierarchy shows the tree from the supplies down, with each panel's connected load.

Cable sizing and voltage drop

Select a device or panel and choose Circuit Properties. It shows the circuit's load, power factor, current, the length of its path from the panel, its cable and its voltage drop. The path is drawn dashed in the view while the circuit is selected. Farthest Device runs square to the axes to the furthest device. All Devices runs from device to device. The path offset raises the horizontal runs, for example into a ceiling void.

Unless you choose a conductor size, Reviera picks the smallest one of the cable type whose voltage drop stays within the limit (3 % unless you change it). It also has to carry the circuit's rating, wherever your ampacity table gives a value for that conductor. A size you choose is kept, and the dialog warns you when it fails. Sizing runs again whenever the circuit, its devices, its path or the settings change.

Reviera does not ship ampacity tables, because code tables can be copyrighted. Enter your code's values under Manage › MEP Settings › Wiring, one row per line (for example copper, pvc, 2_5mm2, 24). That dialog also holds the default cable type, the voltage drop limit, a correction factor for ampacities and the default path offset.

Panel schedules

Select a panel and choose Panel Schedules (Analyze › Reports & Schedules). The schedule opens in a tab and is listed under Panel Schedules in the Project Browser. It shows the panel's supply and slots, then every slot in order: the circuit's number, load name, classification, trip rating, poles, load, and its load on each phase. Slots alternate phases in pairs (1 and 2 on A, 3 and 4 on B, 5 and 6 on C). Below are the totals and currents per phase and the loads summary: the connected load of each load classification, its demand factor and the estimated demand, including the loads of sub-panels.

Type in a Load Name or Trip cell to change the circuit. Load names are made from the loads (for example Lighting – Ceiling Luminaire) until you type one; clearing it goes back. The schedule is live: it changes as soon as the circuits do. Change Panel shows another panel, and Export writes CSV.

Load classifications and demand factors

Manage › MEP Settings › Load Classifications lists the classifications connectors name (Lighting, Power, HVAC, Motor and Other to begin with), each with its demand factor: how much of the connected load is expected at once. New classifications need a unique name. Renaming one also renames it on every connector. Deleting one that connectors use asks which classification they take instead. The five defaults can be renamed but not deleted.

Demand Factors are constant (80 %), by quantity (the number of loads) or by load (their total in VA). Ranges are written as from-to: percent, separated by semicolons, for example 0-10000: 100; 10000-: 50. Incremental applies each range's percentage to the part of the load that falls in it, so 25 kVA becomes 10 + 7.5 = 17.5 kVA. Without it, the whole load takes the percentage of its range. The largest motor rule weights motor loads, largest first (for example 125, 100, 75).

Sizing ducts and pipes

Select a duct or pipe of a system and choose Duct/Pipe Sizing (Analyze › Sizing). Reviera works out each segment's flow from the terminals beyond it, counting from the system's equipment, so the system needs its equipment selected. Then it picks the smallest size from the type's sizing table that meets your targets. Equal Friction keeps friction at or below 1 Pa/m for ducts. Velocity limits the speed, and you can combine the two. Restrictions on height, width or diameter win over the targets: a duct kept at most 300 mm high is widened instead. Branches to terminals can keep the terminal's connector size. A size-locked segment (mep_run.lock_size) is left as it is.

Domestic water is sized for the fixture units beyond each pipe, converted to a flow with your code's fixture-unit table. Import it as a CSV of fixture_units, flow_tank, flow_valve rows under Manage › MEP Settings › Fixture-Unit Table. Reviera does not yet ship a table. Without one, the connectors' own flows are added up and the report says so.

Pressure drop and the loss report

Calculate Networks (Analyze › Pressure Drop) works out the pressure lost in every duct and pipe system, counting from its equipment. Segments lose friction along their length. Fittings lose a share of the velocity pressure: more for a sharp elbow than a gentle one, and more for flow turning into a branch than for flow going straight through. Terminals lose what their connector's loss says, either a coefficient or a fixed pressure drop in Pa. The critical path is the route to the terminal that loses the most, and the equipment has to supply that much pressure. Every other terminal has some excess pressure left over.

Analytical Segments shows one row per segment and fitting, in flow order. Each row gives the flow, velocity, friction, loss, static pressure and whether it is on the critical path. Select Critical Path selects the whole route. Override Loss… replaces a calculated loss with a measured one, or a fitting's coefficient with your own. Reviera keeps the results with the model. Change anything they depend on and they become stale, then they are calculated again the next time you open the table. The Ducts and Pipes schedules can show Flow, Velocity, Friction, Pressure Drop and Critical Path. Schedules also have a Filter… button, for example to list only the critical path.

System Inspector (Analyze › Pressure Drop) shows a calculated system in the view. An arrow on each stretch of duct or pipe shows which way the air or water flows, and the critical path is drawn thick in the warning colour. Hover over a stretch to see its flow, its static pressure where it starts and ends, and what it loses. Properties lists every stretch and how much spare pressure each terminal has. The inspector needs the system's equipment and no open connectors. If any connectors are open, it says how many, and Show Disconnects finds them. It changes nothing in the model, and any edit to the model ends it.

Pressure Loss Report (Analyze › Reports & Schedules) writes a file for the systems you tick. It can be CSV, in SI units, or HTML, in the project's units. Only systems whose type has Calculations set to All can be reported, and the dialog says why any other system can't. Choose the sections you want and save them as a preset so the team uses the same format. Overridden losses are marked in the report. Reviera's fitting coefficients are provisional values awaiting review by an engineer.

Gutters and downspouts

Select a roof and choose Gutter (Systems › HVAC & Piping). Tick the eaves it runs along, in order round the roof. The gutter hangs on the fascia, or at the eave when there is no fascia, is mitred at corners, and falls toward its outlets (1 in 500 unless you set another fall). It belongs to the roof's eaves: change the overhang or the fascia and it moves with them. Select the gutter and choose Gutter Outlet to add an outlet. Its downspout drops from the outlet, bends back to the wall and runs down to the level you pick. The gutter, outlets and downspouts form one Storm Drainage system. After each outlet, Reviera checks the roof area draining to it at 75 mm/h of rain, and warns when the gutter or downspout is too small.

Openings for runs

Where a duct, pipe, cable tray or conduit passes through a wall, floor or ceiling, Reviera opens the host around it automatically, 25 mm clear all round. If you move or resize the run, the opening follows; if you delete the run, the opening goes. Where a run passes through a beam, it is only flagged, because openings in structure need an engineer's approval. Penetrations (Systems › Check Systems) lists every crossing. Select picks a run and its host, and Rules… chooses whether walls, floors and ceilings are opened, flagged or left alone, and sets the clearance.

System types

Every classification has a built-in type of its name with an abbreviation (SA, RA, DCW, …) and a colour. Members of a system are drawn in their type's colour. System Types renames a type, changes its abbreviation and colour, or makes a duplicate, for example a separate Supply Air type for laboratories.

System Browser

System Browser (F9) lists every system by discipline and classification, with its equipment and members, and the connectors that are in no system under Unassigned. Click a node to select its elements. It follows every change to the model.

Checking systems

Check Duct Systems and Check Pipe Systems mark problems in the view with a "!" triangle; the status bar gives the count and the first one:

  • a system not well connected: it has no equipment, or some members are not connected to the equipment;
  • a flow mismatch: the equipment's preset flow differs from its terminals' total, or a calculated outlet has nothing connected;
  • a flow direction mismatch: two connected connectors both flowing in, or both out.

Show Disconnects asks which domains to check and marks every open connector with a red ring until it is connected. Press each button again to turn its markers off. The checks never change the model. mep_check.systems, mep_check.show_disconnects

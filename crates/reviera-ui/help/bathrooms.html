Bathrooms and fixture clearances

Bathroom fixtures need space in front of and beside them: to sit, stand, wash and, for wheelchair users, to approach and transfer. Reviera keeps the rules for that space as rule packs, each from one code, standard or guide, and shows each fixture's clear space as it is placed. Check Bathrooms then lists what stands in that space, which doors swing into it, and whether a WC is the right distance from its side wall. The samples Bathrooms and a home spa, Washrooms and a locker room and Family bathroom and wet room (England) show the tools.

Bathroom components

Place Bathroom Component (Architecture › Build › Bathroom) offers generic fixtures and accessories at their usual sizes and heights, to stand in until products are chosen: WCs (close-coupled, wall-hung, accessible height), a urinal, lavatories (wall-hung, pedestal, hand-rinse, vanity), a bidet, baths, shower trays and a roll-in shower, a mirror, toilet roll holder, soap and paper towel dispensers, a hand dryer, a baby changing unit, a sanitary bin, a coat hook, a folding shower seat and a heated towel rail. Click near a wall: the component goes on its face, its back to the wall, at its own mounting height (a mirror's bottom 1000 mm up, a lavatory's rim 850 mm). Fixtures have their pipe connections, so they join plumbing systems, and their clear spaces and grab bars follow from the rule packs. In IFC they are sanitary terminals, furniture (named Mirror, Shower Seat, …), an electric appliance (the hand dryer) and a space heater (the towel rail).

Bathroom layouts

Bathroom Layout (Architecture › Build) lays out a whole bathroom in a room closed by four walls. Choose a template, then click inside the room in a plan:

  • Full bath (US, 5 × 8 ft): a 60 × 30 in bath across one end, then a WC and a pedestal lavatory along the wet wall.
  • Three-quarter bath: a 900 mm shower tray, WC and lavatory.
  • Powder room, cloakroom WC: a WC and a lavatory or hand-rinse basin.
  • Family bathroom (UK): a 1700 × 700 bath across the end, a WC and a basin.
  • En-suite: a shower tray, WC and hand-rinse basin.
  • Accessible single-user toilet: an accessible-height WC in the corner, a lavatory, and the grab bars the packs ask for.

The fixtures run along the room's longer wall from its left end as you face it (or the right). Each is spaced so that its side clear space under the rule packs in use misses its neighbours, and the first WC is kept within any side-wall distance rule. Under the ADA, a WC's 60 in clearance runs from its side wall, so the next fixture comes after it. If the room is too small nothing is placed, and the message says how many millimetres the wall is short or the room too shallow. Laying out again replaces the earlier layout in that room.

Products in place of placeholders

When a product is chosen, bring in the manufacturer's model and put it where the placeholder was. Make Product… (Architecture › Component, or the Architecture menu) asks for the model file (OBJ, STL, PLY, glTF), the placeholder it replaces, a turn so that its back faces the wall, its units, and the manufacturer's name, model, model reference and article number. The product takes the placeholder's IFC class and pipe connections, and is raised to its mounting height. Its manufacturer data are saved in Pset_ManufacturerTypeInformation.

Select placeholders and choose Swap for Product…. Each keeps its identity, place and data; only its type changes. A report lists each one's size before and after, any connection that moved and by how much (a WC's outlet, for example), connections the product lacks, and the Check Bathrooms findings that appeared or were resolved. Undo puts the placeholders back. A product of another kind (a mirror for a WC) is refused.

A product's documents (data sheet, installation guide, O&M manual, warranty, EPD, HPD, certificates) belong with it. Select a placed product and choose Product Documents…: the Material Documents dialog opens on the product, where you link its folder (on this computer or on FileEngine), set the documents' types, and enter or read values from them. They are saved with the product's type in IFC: the folder as a document reference, the values as property sets. A product's EPD does not make a carbon factor; those are for materials.

Fixture counts

Fixture Count… (View › Coordination, or the Architecture menu) says how many WCs, urinals, lavatories, drinking fountains, showers and service sinks an occupancy needs for its occupant load: IPC Table 403.1 in the US pack (assembly, business, educational, factory, mercantile, residential and storage rows), HSE L24 in the UK workplace pack. The load is split by sex (half each, or the share you give), or counted whole for all-gender rooms (IPC 2021 403.1.1). Ratios such as "one per 25 for the first 50, then one per 50" are applied band by band and rounded up. The result compares each with the fixtures modelled on the plan's level or in the model, gives the shortfall, and says how many of the males' WCs urinals may replace.

Toilet cores

Select the wall a public or workplace toilet stands against, and choose Toilet Core (Architecture › Build). Give the numbers of WCs, urinals and lavatories, or have them counted for an occupancy and load, for a men's, women's or all-gender room (urinals may take the share of the men's WCs the code allows). Along the wall's face come an accessible compartment (60 × 59 in, its WC 18 in from the side partition, with its grab bars), standard compartments (36 × 60 in; under Approved Document T, its types C and D), an ambulatory compartment if asked, urinals 30 in apart with screens between, and lavatories. Partitions are walls of a Toilet Partition type standing 12 in off the floor and 58 in tall; each compartment has an ordinary door in its front panel, swinging out. Only the accessible compartment, one urinal and one lavatory are held to the accessibility pack (bath.set_accessible marks the others); the plumbing code's spacing applies to all. Laying out again replaces the earlier core on that wall, and too short a wall is reported with the shortfall.

Locker rooms

Locker Room (Architecture › Build) lays out a changing room. Give the number of lockers, full height or two tier, the number of gang showers and whether to provide an accessible bench and lockers, then click inside a room closed by walls in a plan. Showers go along one end wall, 750 mm apart, with a 1200 mm drying zone in front; lockers run along both long walls (15 × 18 in, 72 in tall on a 6 in base); 1830 mm benches go down the middle with the aisle the rule packs ask for in front of a locker (760 mm under the ADA, 1500 mm under Approved Document M, else 900 mm); and a 42 × 24 in accessible bench stands against the far end wall with its 30 × 48 in end space clear. At least 5 % of the lockers (at least one) are kept accessible. If they do not all fit, nothing is placed and the message says how many would.

Electrical zones

Under England's building code, the BS 7671 pack draws the zones round each bath and shower where electrical equipment is restricted (shown in yellow with the other virtual volumes): zone 0 inside the bath or tray, zone 1 above it to 2.25 m (or within 1.2 m of the head of a shower with no tray), and zone 2 a further 0.6 m out. Check Bathrooms reports a socket outlet in any zone or nearer zone 1 than 2.5 m, and any other device (luminaire, switch, fan, towel rail, hand dryer) whose IP rating is below what its zone needs (IPX7 in zone 0, IPX4 in zones 1 and 2). Give a device its rating as Pset_ElectricalDeviceCommon.IP_Code (for example IPX4 or IP44); one with none gets a note. The generic heated towel rail is rated IPX4. The values come from a public summary of the 18th edition; shaver sockets, which zone 2 allows, are reported too and can be accepted as intended.

Under the IBC or IRC the us_nec pack checks receptacles instead, after NEC 2023: none within 3 ft (900 mm) of a tub's or shower's outside edge up to 8 ft (2.5 m) above its rim (or the floor, for a shower with no tray), and in a dwelling at least one within 3 ft of each basin. The basin rule waits until some receptacle is modelled, so a plan without its electrical layout is not flagged at every basin. Both sets of zones stop at walls: a socket in the next room, with a wall between it and the bath or shower, is not reported.

Shower falls

Shower Falls (Architecture › Build) lays the falls of a level-access shower: a roll-in shower or a gang shower head with no raised tray, standing on a floor. Select the showers, give the fall in percent (or leave it empty for the rule packs' default: ¼ in per foot, about 2 %, under the IPC; 1:60 under Approved Document M) and choose a drain at the centre or a linear drain along the back. A floor drain is placed, and the floor's top warps so the shower area falls to it while its edge stays at the floor's level; the drain sits deep enough that every part of the area falls at least that much. Run it again to replace a shower's falls. Check Bathrooms reports a level-access shower whose floor has no fall, falls less than the pack's least, or (under Approved Document M, 1:40) more steeply than allowed. A centre drain in a long, narrow shower falls steeply on its short sides; a linear drain falls evenly. The warped floor can be adjusted afterwards with the floor's shape-editing points and lines.

Waterproofing

The rule packs say which walls and floors round a fixture must be waterproof or nonabsorbent. Under the IPC pack: the walls round a shower to 6 ft (1829 mm) and the shower's floor; the walls round a tub to 6 ft (meant for tubs with a shower head); and round a urinal, the wall 4 ft (1219 mm) up and 2 ft (610 mm) past each side, and the floor 2 ft in front. Under Approved Document M, a level-access shower makes the room a wet room: its whole floor and the shower's walls to 1.8 m (practice, not the Approved Document's own words). A wall counts when its face lies along the fixture's back or side. The areas show in blue with the other virtual volumes (set the view's Virtual Volumes to translucent or solid). Waterproofing (Architecture › Build) lists them for the selected fixtures, or all, with each wall or floor, its size and area and the rule, and the totals; answer Yes to copy the schedule as CSV for a spreadsheet. Strips are not yet cut at a wall's ends or openings, and where two fixtures share a wall their strips are counted twice.

Hot tubs

Place Bathroom Component offers two hot tubs, 2.0 m and 2.3 m square. Each type carries its filled weight and seats in the SpaProperties property set (1800 kg and 5 seats, 2400 kg and 7); a product's figures replace them, or set them on a copy with the property editor. Under the IBC or IRC the us_spa pack keeps 0.9 m clear all round a tub for its skirt panels and equipment bay (a wall-hugging tub keeps it on its open sides), and Check Bathrooms reports, as notes since the sources are secondary and differ by edition: receptacles nearer than 6 ft (1.83 m) to the tub, wall switches nearer than 5 ft (1.52 m), and lights or fans over it lower than 7 ft 6 in (2.29 m) above the water (taken 100 mm below the rim). It also works out the filled load with 80 kg a seat: on an upper level, a load above the 40 psf (1.92 kPa) residential floors are commonly designed for is reported for a structural check; on the lowest level the note asks for a pad of at least 100 mm of reinforced concrete. Outdoor spas, barriers and covers are not yet checked.

Saunas

Sauna (Architecture › Build) lays out a sauna. Choose one or two bench tiers, the area of uninsulated surface (a glass door, tile or brick: each m² sizes as 1.2 m³ more), whether the walls are logs (× 1.5), and a heater power if you have one in mind; then click inside a room closed by walls in a plan. Bench modules 600 mm long run along the longer wall: the upper bench 600 mm deep with its top about 1.15 m below the ceiling, the lower 450 mm deep and 450 mm lower. A wall heater goes on an end wall, toward the far side, sized from the room's volume by a maker's table (3 kW for 2–4.5 m³ … 26 kW for 30–66 m³); the status bar gives the power and the design volume. The heater's own safety distances (its product data: 80 mm to the sides and front and 1.1 m to the ceiling for the generic heater) are kept clear like a fixture's clear space. Check Bathrooms reports a heater whose power does not suit the room, too little ceiling over it, a room lower than 2.1 m or higher than 2.44 m, and a door opening into the sauna: sauna doors open outward and have no lock. IRC M1902 also asks for a thermostat limiting the room to 90 °C with its sensor within 152 mm of the ceiling, and a guard round the heater. The tool puts the sensor over the heater 100 mm below the ceiling, and Check Bathrooms reports a sauna without one or with one set too low. The guard is not yet drawn. Choose the Vents, as makers differ: an inlet low beside the heater with the outlet high on the far wall (the default), or the outlet larger and low on the far wall.

Place the sauna's door before you lay it out. With Door left at Vent near its top, the tool puts a 4 × 8 in (203 × 102 mm) vent in each door into the room, 150 mm below its head, as IMC 914 asks. Where the building is not a dwelling (Manage › Model Properties), it also puts a warning notice on the wall outside the door, 1.5 m up: the code asks for one, in ¼ in letters, warning bathers not to stay longer than 30 minutes. Check Bathrooms reports a door with no vent near its top, or too small a one, and outside dwellings a door with no notice outside it.

Steam rooms

Steam Room (Architecture › Build) lays out a steam room. Give the ceiling's high side (2130 mm, 7 ft, by default), the walls' finish and whether one wall is an outside wall, then click inside a room closed by walls in a plan. A ceiling goes over the room, highest over the longer wall and falling 2 in per ft (1:6) to the far wall, so condensate runs down the walls rather than dripping on bathers; a room too deep to keep the low side above 1.8 m is refused. A tiled bench runs along the high wall, the steam head goes low on an end wall across the room from it, and its control 1.5 m up on the far wall. If a floor is under the room, it falls ¼ in per foot to a centre drain. The status bar gives the volume a generator must be chosen for: the room's volume times the finish's factor (tile 1.0, acrylic 0.65, glass 1.35, stone 1.65), plus 10 % a foot above 7 ft and 10 % for an outside wall. These are one maker's factors and others differ, so check the generator's own table. Check Bathrooms reports a steam room with no ceiling or one flatter than 1:6, a room higher than 8 ft, a bench nearer the steam head than 300 mm, and walls without a vapour-proof membrane. Leave Walls on Vapour-proof and the room's walls take the Steam Room Wall (TCNA SR614) type, layered from gypsum board, steel studs, a vapour retarder, cement backer board, a bonded membrane and tile; they keep their location lines, so the room changes size a little.

With Generator left at Outside, a steam generator (an IfcHumidifier, STEAMINJECTION) stands on the floor outside the room, behind the steam head's wall. Makers want it in a dry, ventilated, accessible space, upright on a level floor, and within about 50 ft (15 m) of the head, its steam line pitched back to it with no traps; move it to a closet that suits. Check Bathrooms reports a steam room with no generator, one standing inside the room, and one further from the head in a straight line than 50 ft (the pipe run is longer still), and a control pad lower than 4 ft or higher than 6 ft (1.22–1.83 m).

Accessible saunas, steam rooms and spas

With the ADA in use (View › Coordination › Accessibility Rules), Check Bathrooms asks each sauna and steam room for a 60 in (1525 mm) turning space clear of its benches and heater, and, where it has benches, for a run of benches to §903: 17–19 in high, 20–24 in deep, at least 42 in long. Choose Accessible for the lowest bench in the Sauna or Steam Room dialog to lay one out (24 in deep, its top at 460 mm); the room must be deep enough for the turning space beside the benches. A hot tub in a building that is not a dwelling needs an accessible means of entry by it: place a Spa transfer wall (16–19 in high, 12–16 in deep, 60 in long, with its grab bars) or a Pool lift from Bathroom. A transfer wall of the wrong size is reported.

Whirlpool baths

The Whirlpool bath, 1700 × 750 component has a pump under its foot end. Its position is a property of the bath type (WhirlpoolBath.PumpPosition, metres along the bath from its middle), so a product's own replaces it. Under the IPC and IRC an 18 × 18 in access opening in the apron at the pump is kept reachable: a space 457 mm wide and high and 600 mm deep in front of it shows with the bath's other clear spaces, and Check Bathrooms reports anything standing in it, such as a vanity or a wall built across the apron.

Elevations and schedules

View › Create › Bathroom Elevations, then a click inside a room closed by walls in a plan, makes an interior elevation of each of its four walls: cropped to the room from the floor to the top of its walls, at 1:25, named for the way each looks (Bathroom - North …) and placed two by two on a new A3 sheet. The plan shows them as one marker in the middle of the room, an arrow toward each wall. View › Create › Bathroom Schedules makes a Plumbing Fixture Schedule: each room's fixtures by type with their count and their drainage and water supply fixture units (from the fixtures' pipe connectors) and totals; and a Bathroom Accessories schedule. Place rooms first (Architecture › Room) so the schedules can name them. The fixture units of the generic components are placeholders; a product's connectors carry its own.

Compliance report

View › Coordination › Bathroom Report runs Check Bathrooms and saves its findings as an HTML page, opened in your browser: the rule packs in use and their editions, then each room's findings, errors first, with the rule, what was found, the value against the rule's range, how sure the source is and where it comes from. Issues from Bathrooms makes a BCF issue for each error instead, with a view boxed round the fixture and a snapshot, to assign and track in the Issues pane or export to other BIM tools; running it again only adds new ones, and sets those whose problems have been fixed to Resolved.

Tiles

Architecture › Build › Tiles sets tiles out on a wall or a floor. Select the wall first for a wall. Give the tile size and joint, or leave them for 300 × 600 mm with 3 mm joints on walls and 600 × 600 mm with 5 mm on floors. Choose where to set out from: the middle (a tile centred each way, equal cuts at both ends), the start corner or the end corner (a full tile there). Choose stack or half bond, and for a wall how high to go. Then click in a plan on the side of the wall to tile: the face between the walls meeting it there is tiled. Or click on a floor. Each piece is drawn with its joints, so cut tiles show in plans and in the bathroom elevations, and the status bar counts full and cut tiles, the tiles to order with 10 % for waste, and the area. The layout follows its wall or floor when they change. Tiles stop at the wall's doors and windows, the cut pieces round them counted as cuts; floor tiles follow a floor's falls down to its drain. They are not yet cut round fixtures.

Rule packs

Four packs ship with Reviera. Each rule names its clause, its source and how sure the reading is: verified (read in the official text), secondary (from a reliable summary or an adopted code) or advisory (to be checked).

Clear spaces

Each WC, urinal, lavatory, bidet, bath and shower gets one clear space for each rule of the packs in use. They are virtual volumes, like door swings: they move and turn with the fixture, are never built or scheduled, and show in green when a view shows virtual volumes (View › Coordination › Virtual Volumes). A space is measured across the fixture (from its centreline, an edge or its side wall) and out into the room (from the wall behind it or its front), and runs from the floor to 1.0 m, so that vanities and basins count and things mounted higher do not.

The fixture itself, grab bars, rails and accessories may stand in its space; so may other fixtures' spaces, since clear floor spaces may overlap. Anything else is in the way.

Check Bathrooms

View › Coordination › Check Bathrooms lists the findings, errors first. An error breaks a code or accessibility rule; a note comes from guidance, or from a rule whose reading still needs checking (its confidence is shown in brackets). Pick a finding to select the fixture and what is in its way, frame them, and read the rule, its edition and its source in the status bar. Check Overlaps lists the same intrusions with the other overlaps.

The side wall

Some rules are measured from the wall beside the fixture: the ADA WC clearance runs 60 in from it, and its centreline must be 16–18 in from it. Reviera looks for the nearest wall within 1.2 m either side of the fixture. Where none is modelled yet, it assumes one on the left at the furthest distance the rules allow, and Check Bathrooms says so. To fix the side, select the fixtures and run bath.set_hand with left or right (as you face the fixture), or auto to look again.

Grab bars

A grab bar is a round bar on a wall face, standing off it on a bracket at each end and corner. View › Coordination › Place Grab Bars puts the bars the rule packs ask for at the selected fixtures, or at all of them. Under the ADA, a WC gets a side-wall bar 42 in (1065 mm) long, from 12 in to 54 in off the rear wall, and a rear-wall bar 36 in (915 mm) long, from 12 in on the side-wall side of its centreline to 24 in on the open side; both with their tops 34 in (865 mm) up, 1½ in (38 mm) round and 1½ in off the wall. Placing again replaces the bars placed before. A bar that cannot be placed, for example where no side wall is modelled, is listed with the reason.

To draw one yourself, use grab_bar.create with points on the wall, each with the height of the top of the bar: two for a straight bar, more for an L or U. grab_bar.set changes a bar's diameter, its gap from the wall or its height. Check Bathrooms reports a bar that is missing, does not cover the stretch its rule asks for, is too short, or has its height, diameter or gap out of range. Grab bars are written to IFC as IfcRailing (HANDRAIL), and may stand in a fixture's clear space.

Your own packs

A pack is a JSON file following schemas/bath/reviera.bath-rules-1.schema.json (MIT licensed). To use a local amendment or an office standard, copy a pack from bath.rule_packs, change its values and add it with bath.add_rule_pack. A pack with the id of a shipped one replaces it in this project; bath.remove_rule_pack brings the shipped one back. Project packs are saved with the model.

Limits and what to check

  • Showers are taken as standard roll-in showers under the ADA; a transfer shower's 36 × 48 in clearance is not yet in the pack.
  • The UPC (California) asks for 24 in, not 21 in, in front of a WC, lavatory or bidet: copy the US pack and change it.
  • Approved Document M's Volume 2 values are read from its diagram; ambulant cubicles (920 × 1465 mm since 2024) come with toilet cores.
  • Exceptions that depend on the room (single-user rooms where a door may swing over a clear space, dwelling-unit allowances) are not applied: decide them, and accept the overlap if it is intended.

Overlaps, booleans and clearances

Reviera finds where elements' bodies overlap, and resolves each overlap by accepting it, moving an element apart, or a boolean that is kept up to date. Door swings and accessibility clearances are checked the same way, as virtual volumes.

Checking overlaps

  1. View › Check Overlaps. Check Overlaps
  2. The list shows each overlap, largest first, with the two elements, the overlapping volume and its depth.
  3. Pick one and press OK: the Resolve Overlap dialog follows.

Walls, floors, roofs, stairs, railings, doors, windows and imported elements are checked. Touching is not overlapping, and pairs that belong together by design (a door and its wall, a dormer and its roof, a gate and its railing) are left out. overlap.find

Resolving an overlap

The Resolve Overlap dialog offers, for elements A and B:

Select and frame themSelects the pair and zooms to it, changing nothing.
Accept as intendedRecords the overlap as intended: it no longer appears in the checks.
Cut A by B, Cut B by ASubtracts one body from the other. Reviera asks whether to hide the cutting element's own body: answer Yes when it exists only to shape the cut (like the slot cutter in the Observatory).
Join (by precedence)The element that takes precedence cuts the other: floors and roofs cut walls, walls cut columns.
Keep only what A shares (intersect)A keeps only the part inside B.
Fuse B into A (union)A takes B's volume.
Move B apart, Move A apartMoves one element so they no longer overlap.

The Overlap resolutions sample shows each of these side by side. overlap.resolve, overlap.accept, overlap.accepted

Booleans

Booleans between elements (cut, intersect, union, join) are computed by OpenCASCADE and kept: they are made again whenever either element changes. Besides Check Overlaps, they can be made from the quick modify menu: select exactly two overlapping elements and middle-click one of them.

To take booleans off, select the elements and use View › Coordination › Remove Boolean: their own shapes return. Remove Boolean

In IFC, the shaped bodies are saved with an IfcRelInterferesElements for each boolean. geometry.boolean, geometry.remove_boolean

Clearances and virtual volumes

Every door has a swing volume: the space its leaves sweep. A sliding door has a slide space: the wall its leaves slide over. With the ADA 2010 rule set, doors also get their pull-side and push-side maneuvering clearances. These are virtual volumes: they take part in Check Overlaps, which lists what intrudes into them (and doors that would hit each other), but they are never exported as physical elements.

View › Accessibility RulesChooses the rule set: None (door swings only) or ADA 2010 (door maneuvering clearances, 404.2.4.1). Also in Model Properties. Accessibility Rules
View › Virtual VolumesEach click shows the active view's virtual volumes in turn hidden, translucent or solid. Virtual Volumes

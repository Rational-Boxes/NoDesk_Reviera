Escalators and moving walks

An escalator is laid out from the rise between two floors. Riviera sizes it from the vertical-transport rule packs in use, makes it as one element shaped from that rise, and cuts the floors for its pits and headroom. Check Escalators then says which rule it breaks. The sample Escalators and moving walks in a department store shows the tools.

Placing one

Open a plan of the lower level and choose Architecture › Circulation › Escalator. Choose an escalator or a moving walk and the level it goes up to (None for a horizontal walk on this level, with its length). Choose the step or pallet width, and leave the inclination and flat steps empty for the rule packs' defaults. Then click the middle of its lower end, or type it. At rotation 0 it rises toward the right of the plan.

How it is sized

  • Escalators: 30° unless you give another, 0.5 m/s, and as many flat steps at each landing as the rules ask for the rise and speed. In plan, a 30° escalator with 2 flat steps is 1.732 × the rise + 4.70 m long.
  • Moving walks: 12° between two levels, or horizontal with the length you give, on the floor or set in a pit.
  • The truss, its pits, the handrail distances and the balustrade come from makers' generic sizes. The capacity (persons an hour), the motor's power and the support reactions are planning estimates; a span beyond 16.4 m is noted as needing an intermediate support.

Floor openings

An escalator cuts the lower floor over its pit, and the upper floor from its upper pit as far down the incline as the headroom over the steps reaches the floor's underside. The openings are ordinary shaft openings, deleted with the escalator. They keep the size they were made with: if you change to rule packs with more headroom, Check Clearances shows the floor in the way.

Headroom, side clearance and landings

Each escalator or walk has virtual volumes: its headroom over the steps (2130 mm under A17.1, 2300 mm under EN 115-1), wide enough to keep the side clearance beside the handrails (100 mm or 80 mm), and a landing zone beyond each end. View › Coordination › Check Clearances lists anything in them: a beam, a wall, or a neighbouring escalator too close.

Check Escalators

Architecture › Circulation › Check Escalators checks the inclination (EN 115-1 allows 35° only up to 6 m of rise at 0.5 m/s), the speed, the flat steps and the step width against each pack in use. A17.1's rules come only through state checklists, so its findings are notes.

Limits and what to check

Not yet built: crisscross and scissor arrangements as one command, draft curtains, sprinklers and shutters around the openings, intersection guards, deck barricades, the airport and transit rules (station run-off, unit counts, NFPA 130), and outdoor units.

Scripts and agents

Everything above is a command: escalator.size, escalator.create, escalator.list, escalator.delete and escalator.check; clearance.list and clearance.check include the zones. Lua scripts and AI agents (MCP, the modeling toolset) can use them.

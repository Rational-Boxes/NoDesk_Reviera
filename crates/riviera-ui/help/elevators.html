Elevators

An elevator is laid out from what it must carry. Riviera sizes the car from the vertical-transport rule packs in use, then builds the hoistway round it: a shaft that cuts every floor from the pit to the overhead, hoistway walls, and a sliding entrance at each level it serves. Check Elevators then says which rule a car breaks. The sample Elevators in an office core and Hospital, freight and small lifts show the tools.

Placing an elevator

Open a plan of the lowest level the elevator serves and choose Architecture › Circulation › Elevator. Choose the highest level it serves, and leave the rated load empty for the smallest car the rule packs accept, or type one in kilograms. Choose the drive and speed, the doors, and the rotation: at 0° the landing faces the bottom of the plan. Then click the middle of its entrance, or type the point in the dialog. The status bar gives the sizes it chose.

How it is sized

  • The car is the smallest car in the sizing pack's catalogue that meets every accessibility pack in use. Where the elevator serves enough storeys for a stretcher rule (four under the IBC), it must also take the stretcher, flat, straight or turned. A load you give picks the smallest car of at least that load.
  • The hoistway is the car plus the pack's allowances for the door kind and depth.
  • The pit and overhead come from the pack's table for the drive and speed. A taller car adds its extra height to the overhead.
  • Persons are the load divided by 75 kg.

These are generic, maker-style sizes. A real product's sizes will differ a little; use the maker's planning guide before the core is fixed.

What it is made of

The car is a transport element (IFC IfcTransportElement, type ELEVATOR) on the lowest level, carrying the elevator's capacity, clear size, speed, drive, hoistway, pit and overhead as property sets. The shaft is an ordinary shaft opening, so it also cuts floors added later. With walls, the shaft follows their outer faces, so the floors stop at the hoistway walls. The walls and entrances are ordinary walls and sliding automatic doors. Delete an elevator with elevator.delete, given the car or any of its walls or entrances; it removes them all.

Hospital, freight and small lifts

The dialog's Kind chooses what the elevator is for, and sizing then uses only that kind's catalogue cars, with their own hoistway, pit and overhead:

  • Hospital or service: a 5000 lb car that takes a bed and the stretcher.
  • Freight (class A by default; B, C1, C2 or C3 by script) and Vehicle (class B): no passengers, no landing zones. Check Elevators reports a rated load below the class's least load for the car's area.
  • LULA, Home, Vertical platform lift: swinging entrances that open onto the landing; Check Elevators reports an area, load or rise beyond the kind's limit (a LULA rises at most 25 ft).
  • Dumbwaiter: openings at counter height, 762 mm above each floor.

Freight and vehicle doors (vertical bi-parting) and two-speed side-opening doors are drawn as openings in the hoistway wall until Riviera models them.

Controller, machine space and power

Each elevator gets its controller, and a hydraulic one its power unit, in the space its drive needs, on the side you choose (Controller and machine; None leaves them out):

  • machine-room-less traction: a closet beside the hoistway at the top landing;
  • hydraulic: a pump room beside the hoistway at the lowest landing;
  • traction with a machine room: a room over the hoistway.

The space is a virtual volume (orange) that Check Clearances keeps clear; its walls and door are yours to draw. The controller has a power connector, so it can be put on a circuit and appears in panel schedules. Its load, the motor's power and full-load current and the heat it gives off are planning estimates from the load and speed: no maker publishes these openly. Replace them with the maker's figures when a product is chosen.

Elevator schedule

View › Create › Schedules of Transport Elements lists every car: kind, stops, rated load, persons, speed, drive, car, hoistway, pit, overhead, the machine space, motor power, current and heat.

Panoramic cars and glass hoistways

In the Elevator dialog, Car glass glazes the car's rear, or its rear and sides, and Hoistway can be glass at the sides and rear, on steel posts. The sample Panoramic lifts in a hotel atrium shows both.

  • The car glass is laminated: 10.76 mm (5 + 5 + 0.76) unless a pack asks more. Under EN 81-20 its thickness follows Table 9 by each panel's inscribed circle (8.76 mm up to 1 m, 10.76 mm up to 2 m; beyond, pendulum tests decide), and a handrail 0.9–1.1 m above the floor is noted. The glass is weighed at 2.5 kg per m² per mm, for the cab weight.
  • A glazed traction car's counterweight moves to the side, so its hoistway is 300 mm wider. A hydraulic car has no counterweight and keeps its size.
  • A glass hoistway's sides and rear are curtain walls of 10.8 mm laminated glass on 50 × 110 mm steel posts, at most 1.3 m apart (fixings at most 1.5 m apart, and the largest pane of that glass), with transoms at most 2.5 m apart. The front is a wall with the landing entrances. They are deleted with the elevator.
  • Under the IBC, Check Elevators notes a glass hoistway serving more than two levels: glass in a rated hoistway must be fire-rated glazing, unless the authority accepts the elevator unenclosed, as in an atrium.

Elevator traffic

Architecture › Circulation › Elevator Traffic checks whether each group of elevators moves the building's people fast enough in the morning peak. A group is the passenger elevators serving the same levels at the same speed with the same car. The sample Elevator traffic in an office tower shows one bank that misses the targets and one that meets them.

  • Choose the building's use, and give the people above the lobby; for offices it can be estimated from the floors served at 10 m² a person.
  • Each car carries 80 % of its persons on average, the lesser of its load's persons and its floor area's (0.21 m² a passenger in offices, 0.3 in hotels and homes, 0.4 in healthcare).
  • The report gives the round trip, the interval between cars, the people moved in 5 minutes as a share of the population, and the average wait, against the use's targets (offices: 30 s and 12 %). Where two groups serve the same floors, each needs 120 % of the target.
  • It is a planning check with the textbook up-peak relations: the coefficients are flagged for expert review, and lunchtime or two-way traffic needs a simulation. Escalators' capacity against a demand, with one unit out, is escalator.traffic (no dialog yet).

Elevators in an evacuation

Select an elevator's car, then Architecture › Circulation › Egress Role to mark its part in an evacuation. The sample Evacuation lifts in a tower shows one taken from the top floor.

  • Occupant evacuation elevator (IBC 3008, EN 81-76): people may leave by it. Analyze › Route Analysis › Egress Route with Lifts ticked takes it from the landing in front of its doors on any floor it serves, when that is quicker than the stairs.
  • A ride counts the wait for the car (60 s; lift_wait in a script), 10 s of doors, and the rise at the rated speed with the time lost speeding up and slowing down. These are planning values flagged for expert review.
  • Analyze › Route Analysis › Platform Clearing with Lifts ticked counts evacuation elevators serving the platform and the point of safety's level in its exit capacity (NFPA 130 5.5.6.3.3). One is taken as out of service, and one is kept for firefighters unless a fire service elevator serves the platform. Each counts its rated persons every round trip, and together they give at most half the capacity.
  • Fire service access elevator (IBC 3007, EN 81-72): for firefighters. Routes never take it, and its IFC FireFightingLift property is set.
  • Architecture › Circulation › Check Lobbies checks an evacuation elevator's lobby on each floor it serves (IBC 3008.6). The lobby is the room in front of its landing. It must hold a quarter of the floor's occupants at 3 ft² (0.28 m²) a person, plus a 30 × 48 in wheelchair space for each 50 occupants, and an exit stair must be within 3 m of it. Give the occupants a floor, or leave them to be worked out from the floor area at 150 ft² a person.
  • Standby power, the protected hoistway and the lobby's smoke barrier are not checked yet.

Hoistway and landing zones

Each elevator has virtual volumes: its hoistway (violet), from the pit floor to the overhead, which nothing but the elevator's own parts may enter, and a landing (green) in front of each entrance where a pack gives one: 760 × 1220 mm at the call buttons under the ADA, 1500 × 1500 mm under Approved Document M. View › Coordination › Check Clearances lists what stands in them. Show them by setting a view's virtual volumes to Translucent.

Check Elevators

Architecture › Circulation › Check Elevators checks each car's area against the maximum for its rated load, its size and door against the accessibility packs, and that an elevator serving every floor takes the stretcher where one is needed. Each finding names the rule, pack, edition and source. A finding from guidance or from a rule marked advisory is a note; others are errors. Pick one to select and frame the elevator.

Rule packs

The packs are chosen from the building code (Manage › Model Properties):

  • IBC or IRC: us_ibc_ada (ADA car sizes, the IBC stretcher, landings), us_a17 (ASME A17.1 car area, advisory) and practice_us (sizes);
  • England: en81 (EN 81-20 car area, advisory), en81_70 (type 2 car), england_adm (car, landing, call buttons) and practice_en;
  • otherwise en81, en81_70 and practice_en.

Name others with vt.set_rule_packs, and add your own following schemas/vt/riviera.vt-rules-1.schema.json with vt.add_rule_pack; one with a shipped pack's id replaces it in this project.

Limits and what to check

ASME A17.1 and EN 81-20 were read only through state adoptions, inspection checklists and makers' guides, so their rules are advisory. Not yet built: editing an elevator in place, rear entrances, vertical bi-parting and telescopic doors, machine rooms, pit ladders, hall fixtures, banks of elevators and escalators; a glazed front with glass landing doors, partially enclosed (open) hoistways and their guard heights, counterweight screens, and the solar gain of exterior glass shafts. Power and heat are estimates, and so is the glass hoistway's structure.

Scripts and agents

Everything above is a command: vt.rule_packs, vt.set_rule_packs, vt.add_rule_pack, vt.remove_rule_pack, elevator.size, elevator.create, elevator.list, elevator.delete and elevator.check, elevator.set_egress_role, elevator.check_lobbies; clearance.list and clearance.check include hoistways and landings. Lua scripts and AI agents (MCP, the modeling toolset) can use them.
